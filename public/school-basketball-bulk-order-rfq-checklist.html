School Basketball Bulk Order: RFQ Checklist & Sample Plan

Buying silent basketballs for a PE class, after-school activity, camp or district program? Use this checklist to compare the right equipment, request a sample, and ask suppliers for quotations on the same basis. You can use it with any supplier—no sign-up or purchase required.

1. Match the ball to the activity

Sizes No. 3, 5 and 7. Ask which sizes suit supervised indoor handling, catching and mini-hoop activities. Check MING EAGLE's published model specifications.

Sizes No. 3, 4, 6 and 7. Ask for the exact weight and supervision guidance for the selected variant. Check MING EAGLE's published model specifications.

Flocked indoor soccer ball

No. 5 option for controlled indoor soccer and activity-room programs. Ask about material, dimensions and intended use. Check MING EAGLE's published model specifications.

Important: soft silent balls are not the same as regulation inflated game balls. They are not completely silent and cannot eliminate floor vibration. Do not assume any quantified acoustic rating or safety certification without supporting test documents.

2. Decide on your pilot and bulk quantities

  1. Program: PE class, after-school program, indoor recreation, training clinic or reselling.
  2. Users: approximate age band, activity and adult supervision arrangements.
  3. SKUs: model, size, color and quantity per variant rather than only one combined number.
  4. Sample plan: specify if you want to evaluate one or more real samples before a district order.
  5. Branding: standard ball or requested school/team logo, artwork availability and packaging preference.
  6. Delivery: full destination city/state/ZIP, receiving hours and any district delivery constraints.
  7. Documents: request the specification sheet, carton details, origin, invoice, freight terms and any testing or compliance documents your institution requires.

3. Compare written quotes fairly

Ask each potential supplier to separate the product cost, packaging, freight, import clearance/duties, U.S. sales-tax treatment and final delivered total. Do not assume import duties and U.S. state/local sales taxes are interchangeable. Verify who is responsible for import clearance and any applicable taxes.

Confirm MOQ, carton quantities, whether the products are standard or customized, artwork approval milestones and quote validity. For MING EAGLE, orders of 10 or more units follow a separately approved wholesale quote; the typical sea-freight delivery planning window is approximately 25–30 days and final schedule depends on written terms. Individual small sample orders use separately confirmed air-shipping terms.

Public-school procurement rules vary. Your purchasing office may require a vendor registration, formal solicitation, purchase order or other paperwork before placing an order. We do not claim to be an approved vendor of any district without its confirmation.

Common school buyer questions

Can we request one sample before a large order?

Yes. Request the exact model and size. Availability, sample freight and your destination are confirmed before an order is agreed.

Is the equipment certified for school use?

Do not assume school-specific safety certification or an independent noise-reduction rating. Ask for available technical documentation and have your district assess the suitability for its intended users.

Do you accept district purchase orders or offer tax exemption automatically?

Payment and purchasing arrangements, vendor eligibility and supporting documentation must be assessed for each buyer. A purchase order or exemption request is not automatically approved; any approved Stripe payment option follows an accepted written quote.

Can the bulk order include different sizes or school branding?

Include each requested configuration and the logo artwork in your RFQ. We review what can be supplied, the carton requirements and the corresponding quote before either side commits.

5. Compare silent basketball samples using a repeatable school evaluation

Before buying for a district or after-school program, compare the actual models in a supervised pilot. These are buyer evaluation instructions, not measurements performed by MING EAGLE. Use the same room, floor, participant age group, activity and observation positions for each ball. Any observations are local to that setting, not a laboratory acoustic certificate.

Record the product and unpacking condition

Note the supplier, model, size, material, packaging type, unpacking date and any initial deformation. Take dated photographs with permission. Check shape again after following the actual supplier's recovery and care guidance; do not assume every foam product arrives in the same packaging.

Compare impact sound and floor vibration

With an adult supervisor, use the same controlled handling activity on the same permitted flooring. Have observers note audible thuds in the room and, only with permission, in an adjacent occupied space. Record flooring, contact count and observer position. Phone apps and subjective observations are not certified decibel tests.

Assess rebound, grip and activity fit

Compare the ball's rebound, grip, weight and handling with the activity your program actually intends to teach. Foam silent-style balls need not rebound or respond like regulation air-filled basketballs. Note which drills are appropriate and where a regulation ball is still required.

Check cleaning, surface wear and supervision

Follow the care instructions for each sample. After a short, supervised trial, record visible surface wear, seams, tearing, cleaning practicality and student comfort. If durability matters for a bulk contract, request written material, warranty and replacement terms rather than inventing a product lifespan.

Download the blank school sample evaluation CSV before the trial. It lists four first-party product models but intentionally contains no test results; fill only observations from a permitted, supervised activity. Avoid student names and do not present subjective observations as certified acoustic measurements.

Decision record: For each model, record suitable / needs another trial / not suitable, the reason, the evaluator and the date. Keep product fit, total delivered price, supplier documentation, tax treatment and school approval as separate decisions; a quieter first impression alone does not establish purchasing eligibility.
