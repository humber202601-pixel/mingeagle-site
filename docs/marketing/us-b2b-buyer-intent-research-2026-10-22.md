# MING EAGLE — B2B Buyer-Intent Research Matrix
**Date:** 2026-10-22  
**Purpose:** Spend only on searches with plausible bulk-purchase intent.  
**Status:** Research hypothesis; not live keyword-volume data.

## Executive decision

Do not define a valuable lead as someone who searches for a quiet basketball. Define it as a reachable US buyer with a plausible organization or resale use case and an intended quantity of 10+ units.

The strongest signals are the combination of:
1. **Product fit:** quiet / silent / soft indoor basketball.
2. **Purchase mode:** wholesale / bulk / supplier / distributor / private label / custom logo.
3. **Buyer context:** school / PE program / training academy / sports retailer / distributor.
4. **Lead qualification:** organization, estimated quantity, US delivery destination, model and timing.

A keyword can suggest intent but cannot prove that a searcher is a B2B buyer. The quote form and manual qualification must finish the job.

## 2. Buyer priorities and channel fit

### Priority 1 — Sports distributors, resellers, and e-commerce sellers
- **Why:** A direct wholesale / supplier query is the clearest purchase-mode signal; potential repeat orders.
- **Search signals:** `wholesale`, `bulk supplier`, `distributor`, `private label`, `custom logo`.
- **Landing page:** https://www.mingeagle.com/wholesale-product-catalog.html or https://www.mingeagle.com/wholesale.html
- **Qualification:** company/store URL, intended quantity, models/sizes, resale market, delivery ZIP, expected first-order timing.
- **Caution:** Do not claim factory/manufacturer status unless the company can substantiate it.

### Priority 2 — Training academies, camps, clubs, and indoor activity programs
- **Why:** They may need multiple balls and could make a faster decision than a formal public procurement department.
- **Search signals:** `bulk`, `training`, `academy`, `camp`, `club`, combined with quiet / indoor.
- **Landing page:** https://www.mingeagle.com/wholesale.html
- **Qualification:** number of participants/locations, unit count, intended activity, model and delivery date.

### Priority 3 — Schools and school districts
- **Why:** Product could fit selected indoor PE, after-school, recreation or adapted-activity use cases; quantity potential is meaningful.
- **Search signals:** quiet/soft indoor basketball + school / PE / program / bulk.
- **Landing page:** https://www.mingeagle.com/for-schools.html
- **Qualification:** school/district name, procurement contact, unit count, program use, delivery date, vendor onboarding / purchase-order requirements.
- **Caution:** Public school purchasing often runs through vendor lists, cooperative purchasing arrangements, quotes or formal solicitations. It can have a longer sales cycle than a small private buyer. Treat public procurement as a parallel account-development channel, not as a promise of quick ad conversions.

Evidence of formal buying paths:
- Sourcewell / School Health lists athletic and PE equipment categories, contract documentation and supplier contacts: https://www.sourcewell-mn.gov/cooperative-purchasing/050924-SHC
- Needham Public Schools lists athletic / PE bid vendors and state contract routes: https://www.needham.k12.ma.us/departments/business__operations/business_office/purchasing/procurement_information
- Fairfax County Public Schools describes vendor registration and procurement routes: https://www.fcps.edu/get-involved/procurement-services

### Priority 4 — Government / public agencies
- Potentially large orders, but procurement eligibility, vendor registration, documentation, bid schedules and contract requirements can be significant barriers.
- Do not spend initial search budget on generic government procurement keywords. First map required vendor registrations and identify solicitations that explicitly fit quiet/soft indoor balls.
- A public bid for standard basketballs does not establish a need for a quiet basketball. Confirm the technical requirement before pursuing it.

Start with these candidates in Keyword Planner:

## 4. Negative keyword policy

### Safe initial exclusions (clearly unrelated intent)
- jobs
- career
- salary
- DIY
- how to make
- repair
- used
- second hand
- free printable
- game download
- NBA scores
- basketball video game
- court rental

Do **not** add `free`, `kids`, `school`, `training`, `foam`, `mini hoop`, `cheap`, or `price` as blanket negatives before reviewing real search terms. Some could describe a relevant buyer, a product feature, or an inquiry.

Google warns that negative keyword matching differs from positive keyword matching and does not automatically cover every close variant; maintain singular/plural and phrase exclusions intentionally. Official guide: https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972

## 5. How we will decide whether a keyword survives

Use the first 14-day capped test to answer these questions, not to chase click volume.

| Signal | Keep / expand | Pause / exclude |
|---|---|---|
| Search term | Contains credible wholesale, supplier, institutional or program intent and matches the actual quiet/soft product | Unrelated game, job, repair, used goods or consumer-only intent |
| Click | Visitor reaches the intended buyer page | Don't treat a click as a lead |
| Inquiry | CRM accepts a submission and contact is reachable | Spam, duplicate, unreachable or accidental submission |
| Qualified RFQ | Real organization/use case and intended quantity of 10+ units | Individual sample order or no plausible bulk requirement |
| Quote | Supplier can meet requested model, quantity, shipping and terms | Product or shipping cannot meet requirement |
| Sale | Payment is verified in Stripe | Unpaid quote or verbal interest |

After each few days of delivery, review the Search Terms report. Add negatives only when the query is clearly irrelevant; don't block an entire buyer category because of one weak search.

## 6. Measurement and unit economics

Primary KPI: **cost per qualified B2B RFQ**, not cost per click.

Also record:
- qualified RFQs by buyer type and ad group;
- quote-sent rate;
- quote acceptance rate;
- Stripe-verified paid orders;
- order contribution after product cost, packaging, shipping, fees and any tax obligations;
- time from first click to RFQ and RFQ to order.

For the first test, keep the GA4 lead event as a diagnostic signal until it is verified end-to-end. Later, if the CRM can reliably identify qualified leads and closed orders, consider importing qualified / converted lead outcomes into Google Ads. Google documents these as offline stages in the lead journey: https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10029210

## 7. Evidence gaps to close before spend

1. Keyword Planner search volumes, historical trends, competition and top-of-page bid ranges for US / English.
2. The actual billing blocker in Google Ads.
3. Real consented test submission visible in GA4 and successful import into Google Ads.
4. Whether custom-logo, sample and freight terms on the landing pages match current operating capacity.
5. A simple manual lead-qualification status in the CRM: New → Contacted → Qualified → Quote sent → Won / Lost.

Keyword Planner's official documentation states that it provides location/language-specific keyword ideas, average monthly searches and cost estimates; basic access requires billing information. Until that data is available, don't invent volume or CPC:
- https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243
- https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6325025

## Final recommendation

Start with explicit wholesale intent and product-specific institutional intent. Keep generic consumer searches out of the initial paid test. Treat schools as a strong but potentially slower segment, and develop public procurement leads through a separate vendor / solicitation workflow. The campaign should only be enabled after billing, budget cap, landing-page fit and conversion measurement are verified.
